Keynote Speaker I

Title : Impedance-based Immunosensor for Point-of-care Diagnostic Testing

Abstract :

An ultrasensitive and real-time impedance based immunosensor chip has been fabricated for the quantitative detection of Galectin-1 (Gal-1) protein, a biomarker for the onset of multiple oncological conditions, especially bladder cancer. The chip consists of a gold annular interdigitated microelectrode array (3x3 format with a sensing area of 200 µm) patterned using standard microfabrication processes, with the ability to electrically address each electrode individually. To improve sensitivity and immobilization efficiency, we have utilized nanoprobes (Gal-1 antibodies conjugated to alumina nanoparticles through silane modification) that are trapped on the microelectrode surface using programmable dielectrophoretic manipulations. The immunosensor can detect Gal -1 protein in T24 cell lysate spiked phosphate buffered saline, artificial urine and human urine samples with a limit of detection estimated to be in the pg/ml range. The normalized impedance variations show a linear dependence on the concentration of cell lysate present while specificity is demonstrated using unspiked control samples. We have also designed a portable impedance analyzing device to connect the immunosensor for regular check up in point of care testing with the ability to analyze results from a cloud database using a mobile app or a personal computer. We believe that this diagnostic system would allow for improved public health monitoring and aid in early cancer diagnosis.

Prof. Dr. Cheng-Hsin Chuang
Department of Mechanical Engineering,
Southern Taiwan University of Science and Technology, Tainan 71005, Taiwan
Biography:

Cheng-Hsin Chuang received the B.S. degree and Ph.D. degree from National Cheng Kung University in 1995 and 2002, respectively, both in Civil Engineering. He then held Postdoctoral research scholarship with Center for Micro/Nano Science and Technology at NCKU, where he was in charge of the core facilities for MEMS fabrication and Nanotechnology. Since 2005, he was recruited by Department of Mechanical Engineering and Institute of Nanotechnology at Southern Taiwan University of Science and Technology as an Assistant Professor. Currently, he is a full Professor and serves as the Director of Roll-to-Roll Imprinting Center for Flexible OptoElectronics (RicFoe) and the Micro and NanoSensing Technology Lab (MANST Lab).



Keynote Speaker II

Title : Toward Smarter Power Grid: New Stabilizing Control Concept

Abstract :

Nowadays, the massive increase in power demands with insufficient energy resources causes the critical problem of energy security. To serve such power consumptions, electric power systems are required to operate near their stability limits. In addition, the penetration of renewable energy sources with intermittent power output such as wind and solar into power grids highly escalates. Moreover, catastrophic events such as extreme weather, and natural disasters, etc. tend to increase inevitably. These mentioned problems negatively impact on the power system stability. Without effective countermeasures, the system stability may not be maintained. As a result, wide area blackouts may occur. To tackle above problems, new stabilizing control techniques are significantly required. This presentation aims to provide new concept about smart grid stabilizing control. Wide area stability monitoring and control based on GPS-synchronized phasor measurement units, superconducting magnetic energy storage with fault current limiting function, and wind turbine with power oscillation damper will be discussed.

Prof. Dr. Issarachai Ngamroo
King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang, KMITL, Thailand
Biography:

Issarachai Ngamroo is a Professor at the Department of Electrical Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang (KMITL), Bangkok, Thailand. He received the B.Eng degree in electrical engineering from KMITL in 1992. He earned the Japanese government scholarship to continue his study at Osaka University in 1993, and received the M.Eng and PhD degrees in electrical engineering, in 1997 and 2000, respectively. He joined Sirindhorn International Institute of Technology, Thammasat University in 2000. He served as assistant professor and associate professor in 2001 and 2004, respectively. In 2007, he joined Department of Electrical Engineering, KMITL. Since February 2012, he has served as Professor in Electrical Engineering. His research interests are in the areas of power system stability, dynamics, and control.

Dr. Ngamroo served as the General Chairman of the 2010 and 2012 IASTED Asian Conference on Power and Energy System, the 2014 AUN/SEED-Net Regional Conference on Energy Engineering. He was an associate editor of the ECTI Transactions on Electrical Engineering, Electronics, and Communications during 2005-2015.

Currently, Dr. Ngamroo is the Editor-in-Chief of Ladkrabang Engineering Journal. He also serves as a member of Thailand Smart Grid Development committee, Energy Policy and Planning Office, Ministry of Energy, Thailand.
